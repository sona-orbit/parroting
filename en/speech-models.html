Speech recognition models

Pick a model that understands you.

Parroting is not tied to any one speech recognition model. During setup you pick one of the open models below to download; later you can switch any time from Manage models on the Speak page. Whichever you choose, recognition runs on your Mac and your voice is never uploaded.

Not sure which one?

Three common starting points. The one marked “Recommended” in the app is SenseVoice Small.

Fast and smallSenseVoice Small

About 250 MB and the fastest multilingual model here. Runs smoothly on older Macs too.

Most accurate ChineseQwen3-ASR 1.7B

The fewest Chinese errors on this list, at about 1.5 GB and slower. Qwen3-ASR 0.6B is a middle ground.

Taiwan Mandarin with English mixed inBreeze-ASR-25

Tuned by MediaTek Research for Taiwan Mandarin and code-switching. The slowest, but it knows Taiwanese usage better.

What each model is good at

SenseVoice SmallFunAudioLLM (Alibaba) · 253 MB

Small and the fastest; it detects whether you are speaking Chinese, Cantonese, English, Japanese or Korean. A few more Chinese errors than the larger models.

Qwen3-ASR 0.6BQwen (Alibaba) · 850 MB

30 languages, strong on Chinese and Cantonese, a good balance of accuracy and speed.

Fun-ASR NanoFunAudioLLM (Alibaba) · 891 MB

A newer FunAudioLLM model for Chinese, English and Japanese; Chinese accuracy close to Qwen3-ASR 0.6B.

Breeze-ASR-25MediaTek Research · 1.2 GB

Built on Whisper and tuned for Taiwan Mandarin and code-switching. The slowest on this list.

Qwen3-ASR 1.7BQwen (Alibaba) · 1.5 GB

The larger Qwen3-ASR; the most accurate here for Chinese and Cantonese, and it uses more memory.

Fun-ASR Nano MultilingualFunAudioLLM (Alibaba) · 891 MB

The multilingual Fun-ASR Nano: 31 languages, very accurate for Chinese and Japanese.

Whisper Large v3 TurboOpenAI · 886 MB

OpenAI’s multilingual model with the most languages; weaker on Cantonese.

Moonshine Tiny (Chinese)Moonshine AI · 35 MB

Tiny, about 35 MB, Chinese only. Noticeably less accurate; good when space is tight or just to try things out.

Moonshine Base (Chinese)Moonshine AI · 77 MB

About 77 MB, Chinese only. In this benchmark it is not more accurate than Tiny.

SenseVoiceFunAudioLLM (Alibaba) · 160 MB

An older-format build of SenseVoice Small (ONNX int8, runs on the CPU); same model and license. No public benchmark figures.

Breeze ASRMediaTek Research · 1.1 GB

An older-format build of Breeze-ASR-25 (ggml q5_k); same model and license. No public benchmark figures.

Use your own model

Models outside this list work too: any .gguf file of a kind Parroting supports (such as SenseVoice, Qwen3-ASR, Fun-ASR, Whisper or Moonshine). On the Speak page, click Manage models, then Import model… next to Downloaded Models. Parroting test-recognizes a sentence first and only switches once it gets the words; you can switch back any time.

Licenses

You download models right in the app, no files to hunt for: pick one during setup, and get others later from Manage models on the Speak page. In the Source column, “Original” is the developer’s open-source page and “.gguf file” is the converted version the app downloads, if you want the details.

Each model has its own license, set by its developer; click a license name in the table to read it. Read it before you use or fine-tune a model; anything you train from these models still follows the original license.

You download these models from third-party hosts (Hugging Face and Handy’s mirror, blob.handy.computer) and use them under their own licenses; Parroting does not distribute or modify them and does not represent their developers. The .gguf conversions are provided by the Handy project (handy-computer).

A model you import yourself is used under the license of its source.

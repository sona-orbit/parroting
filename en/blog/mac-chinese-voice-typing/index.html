Voice Typing on Mac: Dictation, AI Cleanup & Translation

Start with the dictation already built into macOS, then decide whether a more flexible voice workflow will save you time.

1. Start with built-in macOS Dictation

You may not need another app. Open System Settings, find Dictation, and enable it if supported. Place your cursor in Notes, Mail, or another text field, then use your configured dictation shortcut. Keyboard shortcuts, supported languages, and network requirements vary by macOS version and setup. For an occasional short note, this might be all you need.

2. Dictate where you're already working

For frequent writing, the friction often comes from switching apps and copying transcripts. Parroting supports holding Right Option + Right Command while speaking, then releasing to insert the recognized text at your cursor. Continuous dictation is also available. Grant the macOS permissions requested by the app, and test compatibility with the software you use every day.

3. Clean up filler words and corrections

Spoken language naturally includes pauses, filler words, and mid-sentence changes. For example: “Let's meet at three—sorry, three thirty. I'll send the files tonight.” A cleaned-up version could be “Let's meet at 3:30. I'll send the files tonight.” This is an illustrative example, not an actual model test. Parroting offers optional AI speech cleanup, but you should still verify dates, names and figures.

4. Speak Chinese, produce English text

For multilingual work, drafting in Chinese, opening a translator, copying the result, and switching back can be tedious. Parroting's translation rewrite mode turns recognized speech into an English draft that you can review before applying. Speech recognition and AI translation are separate steps: translation may require your chosen AI provider and an API key.

5. Review a message's tone before sending

A direct request can sound accusatory when it arrives as plain text. Parroting provides tone reminders and rewrite suggestions, including controls for warmth and formality. Instead of “Why haven't you sent the files again?”, you might choose “Could you let me know when the files will be ready?” You decide whether to use or edit the suggestion; tone checking cannot replace human judgment.

6. Teach it the names you use

Names, specialized vocabulary, and mixed-language terms can trip up speech recognition. Parroting includes a personal dictionary and a correction-review process, plus local personalization training you can choose to adopt. It does not guarantee perfect transcription. Try your frequently used words and compare results for yourself.

7. Choose based on your actual needs

For occasional dictation, try macOS first. If you're composing messages across several apps every day, compare shortcut-based insertion and continuous listening. If translation and tone editing matter to you, try the optional AI-assisted steps. Parroting's speech recognition and personalization primarily run on the Mac, while optional rewriting can send text to your configured external provider. The current download targets Apple silicon Macs on macOS 11 or later.

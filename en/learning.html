Layer 1

1. Dictionary: Fix it once, get it right next time

The most direct way Parroting learns. No model training required—just saving custom words and corrections.

Edits go straight to review

When you correct a misheard word, Parroting lists the “Before → After” fix on the Personalization page’s review list. You click “Add to dictionary” to confirm, preventing ordinary phrasing changes from being mislearned as speech errors.

Add your own

You can also go to Settings → Words & history → "Add", and enter the correct spelling along with common mishearings.

What it is best for

Names, product names, acronyms, and technical terms (for example, Gemini misheard as Jmini).

What it cannot fix

Homophones that depend on context (such as “their” vs “there”); leave those to a custom speech model.

Layer 2

2. Tone personalization: Reminders that match your boundaries

Everyone has different standards for what counts as direct versus blunt. Parroting lets you teach it your personal line.

Trained on top of the base model

The built-in tone model is just the starting point. When you click “Hint was wrong” on a reminder card and confirm it under Personalization in the sidebar, you can start training once you reach 20 confirmed items. Parroting trains a personalized adjustment layer on top of the original model right on this Mac, and your data is never uploaded.

Double verification safeguards

1 in 5 items is held out from training specifically for validation; built-in benchmark sentences run in parallel to ensure overall accuracy does not degrade. If it fails validation, it cannot be adopted.

Version history and rollback

Every adopted version is saved in your history, so you can revert to a previous version or back to the original model at any time.

Works in English and Chinese

Tone personalization works for both Chinese and English.

Layer 3 · Advanced

3. Custom speech model (Advanced): Train your ears with your recordings

Speech recognition in Parroting runs locally on your Mac. The built-in models work well for most people, but everyone has a unique accent, speaking speed, and vocabulary. If you have a computer with an NVIDIA GPU, you can fine-tune a personal version with your own recordings and import it into Parroting.

Real test results

Tested twice with the developer’s own voice recordings (SenseVoice Small, training only ~2% of parameters):

StageAudio lengthError rate (unseen test recordings)
Original model—≈5.0%
First training30 min≈3.6%
Second training≈2 h≈3.4%
  • ~30% fewer errors, with the biggest improvement in frequently used technical terms and accent-specific phrases.
  • 4× more audio yielded only marginal gains. The reason: both runs used pseudo-labels estimated by another model, which contained errors; further gains require truly accurate text, such as reading from a script or confirming your own corrections.
  • Tradeoff: The model becomes tailored to you, but performs about 1 point worse on the general test set.

Steps

1

1. Keep recordings

Parroting keeps the latest 300 recordings by default. To collect more training data, go to Settings → Advanced → More settings → History → "Keep recordings" and choose a longer retention period.

2

2. Prepare accurate transcripts

The most reliable transcripts come from reading a prepared script aloud or reviewing your corrections inside Parroting. Training on raw machine recognition works, but reaches an accuracy plateau (see table above).

3

3. Export

Go to Settings → Words & history → My data → "Export". You will get a zip containing history and recordings, iCloud text records, dictionary, my data, and personalization material.

4

4. Train

On a PC with an NVIDIA GPU, fine-tune SenseVoice Small with LoRA (starting from the original checkpoint, training only ~2% of parameters).

  • Reference: ~1,000 clips / 2 h of audio takes ~3.5 h for 4 epochs on CPU; a GPU will be significantly faster.
  • Hold out a slice of audio as an evaluation set and test before and after training to verify real improvement.
  • Use Simplified Chinese for training text: in testing, training with Traditional Chinese transcripts degraded performance. Parroting converts output to Traditional Chinese automatically when set to Traditional Chinese.
5

5. Convert to Parroting's format

Convert to GGUF using transcribe.cpp's convert-sensevoice.py, then quantize with transcribe-quantize --quant Q8_0 down to ~250 MB.

6

6. Import

Go to Settings → Talk → Manage models → "Import model…", and select the .gguf file. Parroting tests recognition on a trial sentence first, only enabling the model if words are recognized; click "Back to the standard model" at any time if you want to switch back.

Notes

License: SenseVoice Small is released under the FunASR Model License; any model trained from it must also comply with the original license.

Privacy: Exported data contains your voice and spoken words. Please exercise caution before sharing it with others.

Training on a Mac: Training directly on a Mac has not been verified yet; a computer with an NVIDIA GPU is recommended.

Layer 1

Misheard once, right next time

When you fix a misheard word, Parroting suggests adding it to the dictionary. It is added only when you confirm.

Details
  • You can also add words yourself: Settings → Words & history → "Add", with the right spelling and common mishearings.
  • Best for names, product names and technical terms.
  • It cannot fix sound-alike words that depend on context; your own speech model helps there.

Layer 2

Hints that match your sense of tone

If a hint is off, press "Hint was wrong". After 20 items, Parroting trains a layer of judgment that is only yours, on your Mac.

Details
  • Confirm your feedback on the "Personalization" page in the sidebar. Nothing is uploaded.
  • One in five items is held back to check the result, plus a built-in check set; if the new layer does worse, it is not used.
  • Every adopted version is kept, so you can go back one version or to the original model.
  • Works in English and Chinese.

Layer 3 · Advanced

Your recordings, your ear

With an NVIDIA GPU you can train your own speech model on your recordings and import it into Parroting. Two runs by the developer:

Original model~5.0%
30 min audio~3.6%
~2 h audio~3.4%

About 30% fewer errors, mostly on names and accent words you use a lot. The trade-off: slightly worse on other people's speech (about 1 point).

  1. Keep recordingsSettings → Advanced → More settings → History → "Keep recordings"
  2. ExportSettings → Words & history → My data → "Export"
  3. TrainWith the open-source parroting-asr-training, about 8–9 min on an RTX 4090
  4. ImportSettings → Talk → Manage models → "Import model…"
For people who train: full steps and notes
  • Correct transcripts matter most: read a script aloud, or use corrections you confirmed in Parroting. Raw recognition works too, but hits a ceiling (4× more audio in the second run barely helped).
  • The training kit reads the "Export training data…" format from the next Parroting version (coming soon); the current full "Export" zip does not work with it yet. 8 GB GPUs work. MIT license.
  • It fine-tunes SenseVoice Small with LoRA, about 2% of the parameters. About 1,000 clips / 2 h of audio take ~3.5 h for 4 epochs on CPU; a GPU is much faster.
  • Keep some recordings out of training as a test, and test before and after.
  • For Chinese, use Simplified Chinese transcripts: Traditional transcripts made it worse in testing.
  • Convert: transcribe.cpp convert-sensevoice.py to GGUF, then transcribe-quantize --quant Q8_0 to about 250 MB.
  • On import, Parroting tries one sentence and enables the model only if it recognizes words; "Back to the standard model" any time.
  • License: SenseVoice Small is under the FunASR model license, and so is anything trained from it.
  • Privacy: the export holds your voice and what you said; think before sharing it.
  • Training on a Mac has not been verified yet; use a computer with an NVIDIA GPU.
